# Tokyo Transport on a Budget: Metro Passes, Discount Tickets & IC Cards

Tokyo's train network is the best on the planet — and surprisingly affordable if you use the right passes instead of tapping your IC card every ride.

The city is served by several independent operators — **Tokyo Metro**, **Toei (Toei Subway)**, **JR East**, and a handful of private lines (Tobu, Keikyu, Tokyu, Seibu…). They overlap on the map but bill separately. Almost every "saving" in Tokyo comes from understanding that fact.

## Unlimited metro passes (the best value)

### Metro & Gurutto Pass (メトロ＆ぐるっとパス)
Tokyo Metro sells a combined pass covering **all Tokyo Metro + Toei lines + JR East within the 23 wards + trams and buses** for 24 or 72 hours. If you're sightseeing across the city, this single ticket beats pay-per-ride by a mile.

- **Official announcement & prices:** [Tokyo Metro news page](https://www.tokyometro.jp/news/2024/217686.html)
- **Detailed PDF (Japanese and English):** [Metro pass leaflet](/blog/metro-pass-leaflet-2024.pdf)

### When does a pass pay off?

A single metro ride costs ¥180–220. A 24-hour pass costs roughly that ×7–8. So:

| Your day | Best option |
|---|---|
| 3–4 rides between 2–3 different operators | Pay per ride |
| 5+ rides crossing Metro/Toei/JR | **24h Metro & Gurutto Pass** |
| Full city-circuit sightseeing day | **Gurutto Pass** — JR *and* Metro *and* Toei |
| Sightseeing confined to one operator's lines | That operator's own 1-day pass (Tokyo Metro: ¥600, Toei: ¥700) |

The trap: a Tokyo Metro–only pass doesn't work on Toei, and vice versa. Tourists on a Tokyo-Metro day pass who try a JR ride get bounced at the ticket gate — that's the single most common transit mistake in Tokyo. The Gurutto Pass exists precisely because operators don't share.

## Cheap Tickets & IC Cards

[GoTokyo's guide to Cheap Tickets & IC Cards](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/plan/getting-around/ic-card/index.html) covers the basics: Suica / Pasmo cards, airport express deals, and one-day passes.

IC cards in practice:

| Card | Where to get | Works on |
|---|---|---|
| **Suica** | JR stations (welcome Suica at Haneda/Narita, airport kiosks) | All Tokyo operators, konbini, vending machines |
| **Pasmo** | Metro/Toei stations & airport | Same network as Suica (interoperable) |
| **Child IC card** | JR ticket office, with passport | Half fares — 6–11 years |

Functionally Suica and Pasmo are interchangeable for Tokyo travel. Pick whichever sells without a queue at your arrival station.

## Save money with these habits

1. **Get a Suica/Pasmo IC card on day one** — every station, konbini and vending machine accepts it
2. **Buy one-day passes for heavy-sightseeing days** (¥700–1,000 instead of repeated ¥180–200 rides)
3. **Check the fare map before riding** — changing lines between operators (Tokyo Metro vs. JR vs. Toei) doubles the fare
4. **Walk one station** — Tokyo station spacing is short; a 15-minute walk often replaces a ¥180 ride
5. **Avoid peak-hour taxis entirely** — a 3 km ride costs more than a full day of metro passes
6. **Ride at night without a monthly pass** — many stations offer discounted evening fare zones on private lines
7. **Ask about "commuter pass" (定期券, teiki-ken)** if you stay a month+ — a fixed route monthly pass often costs less than 20 individual tickets

### The teiki-ken reality for long-stay learners

If you commute 4+ days/week on the same route for a month or longer, a **teiki-ken (regular commuter pass)** is usually cheaper than every alternative: unlimited rides on that fixed route plus 50% off any ride entering or leaving it. You'll need your passport (and for 3+ months, the application form from a station with a "Midori no Madoguchi" counter). Price example: a 1-month pass covering a 5-station commute costs roughly ¥8,000–12,000 — meaning it pays for itself in about 35 rides.

## ⚠️ The last-train rule (saves you a ¥5,000 taxi)

> **Tokyo trains don't run all night. Local lines stop at every station — but Express or Rapid Express trains SKIP stations at night. Double-check your platform and time before boarding.**

The last trains leave central stations around **23:55–00:30**. Miss it and you're looking at a taxi (¥4,000–7,000 for any real distance) or waiting until ~04:30.

Two practical safeguards:

- Open your map app and look at the **final train before 24:00**, not the *next* train. On shared apps the last connection often appears one hour later than the physical train — because of the transfer time.
- If you miss it in Shibuya/Shinjuku, one-night capsule hotels from ¥3,000 are frequently cheaper than a taxi and give you a shower.
