# Tokyo Transport on a Budget: Metro Passes, Discount Tickets & IC Cards

Tokyo's train network is the best on the planet — and surprisingly affordable if you use the right passes instead of tapping your IC card every ride.

## Unlimited metro passes (the best value)

### Metro & Gurutto Pass (メトロ＆ぐるっとパス)
Tokyo Metro sells a combined pass covering **all Tokyo Metro + Toei lines + JR East within the 23 wards + trams and buses** for 24 or 72 hours. If you're sightseeing across the city, this single ticket beats pay-per-ride by a mile.

- **Official announcement & prices:** [Tokyo Metro news page](https://www.tokyometro.jp/news/2024/217686.html)
- **Detailed PDF (Japanese and English):** [Metro pass leaflet](/blog/metro-pass-leaflet-2024.pdf)

## Cheap Tickets & IC Cards

[GoTokyo's guide to Cheap Tickets & IC Cards](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/plan/getting-around/ic-card/index.html) covers the basics: Suica / Pasmo cards, airport express deals, and one-day passes.

## Save money with these habits

1. **Get a Suica/Pasmo IC card on day one** — every station, konbini and vending machine accepts it
2. **Buy one-day passes for heavy-sightseeing days** (¥700–1,000 instead of repeated ¥180–200 rides)
3. **Check the fare map before riding** — changing lines between operators (Tokyo Metro vs. JR vs. Toei) doubles the fare
4. **Walk one station** — Tokyo station spacing is short; a 15-minute walk often replaces a ¥180 ride

## ⚠️ The last-train rule (saves you a ¥5,000 taxi)

> **Tokyo trains don't run all night. Local lines stop at every station — but Express or Rapid Express trains SKIP stations at night. Double-check your platform and time before boarding.**

The last trains leave central stations around **23:55–00:30**. Miss it and you're looking at a taxi (¥4,000–7,000 for any real distance) or waiting until ~04:30.
