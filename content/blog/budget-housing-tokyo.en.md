# Budget Housing in Tokyo: Share Houses & Apartments

Tokyo rent is famously brutal — but only if you insist on living alone in a private apartment straight away. For your first months (or your whole stay), **share housing** cuts your rent in half and gives you an instant social circle.

## Share house vs. private apartment

| | Share house | Private apartment |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly rent** | ¥50,000–¥90,000 (incl. utilities often) | ¥80,000–¥150,000 (central Tokyo) |
| **Move-in costs** | None or ¥10,000–30,000 | Key money, deposit, agent fee = 4–6 months' rent |
| **Furniture** | Fully furnished | Usually empty |
| **Contract length** | Monthly rolling | 2 years (renewal fee) |
| **Social life** | Instant community | You're on your own |
| **Privacy** | Shared kitchen/bath | Full |

The move-in math alone decides most cases: a private apartment in central Tokyo typically requires **4–6 months' rent upfront** (key money/reikin, deposit/shikikin, agent fee/chukai tesuryo plus first month). That's ¥400,000–900,000 before you sleep in it once. A share house starts at the first month's rent.

## The three-month decision path

Almost everyone converges on this sequence, even when they arrive expecting to rent directly:

1. **Months 0–1: a share house, dorm or guesthouse.** You need only the first month's rent, and you learn what "convenient" actually means in your Tokyo life.
2. **Month 2–3: pick your real neighborhood** — the one you *demonstrably* use, not the one you imagined from a map. This alone prevents the classic expensive mistake of signing a 2-year lease for a commute you end up hating.
3. **From month 3+:** if you're still in Japan and know your area, upgrade to a private apartment with a proper contract — or stay in the share house, which many long-term residents do deliberately.

## Recommended share house sites

### [Share Share (シェアシェア)](https://share-share.jp/)
The biggest share house search site in Japan. Tip: use your browser's auto-translate to navigate in English.

### [Tokyo β](https://www.tokyobeta.jp/en/)
A personal favorite — Tokyo β runs modern share houses with strong IT-engineer communities, and their pricing is one of the best deals in the city. English site available.

### [XROSS HOUSE](https://x-house.co.jp/en/)
Dozens of locations across Tokyo, many women-only houses, no deposit and no key money. English-friendly.

### [Shares (シェアーズ)](https://shares.house/)
Designer share houses with good locations. Tip: use auto translate — the site is in Japanese.

## What to watch for

- **Utilities:** some houses include them in rent, some don't — that difference can be ¥10,000/month
- **Private room vs. dorm:** a private room costs more; dorms (bunk beds) are the cheapest
- **Minimum stay:** some houses want 1–3 months minimum — check before applying
- **Guest policy:** many share houses forbid overnight guests
- **What "cleaned" means in the contract** — in shared Japanese houses, cleaning duty is often a rotating chore ("souji-toban") rather than a paid service
- **Trash rules are stricter than you think** — burnable/plastic/non-burnable/collection-day schedules differ by ward and are enforced by your neighbours, loudly

## Where in Tokyo the value really is

Rent depends less on distance than on the number of *direct central lines* from your station. These areas are the persistent value picks:

| Area | Why it's cheap | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **West-side: Koenji, Asagaya, Ogikubo** | 10–15 min to Shinjuku on the Chuo line | Real neighbourhoods, cheap izakayas, actual Japanese life |
| **North-side: Ikebukuro surrounds, Itabashi, Wako** | Less central than Shibuya | Big stations, direct central access, rents ¥30,000 lower |
| **East-side: Koiwa, Funabashi border** | Cheapest with a working direct train to central Tokyo | Longest commute, but rent can halve |
| **South-side: Mita, Gotanda, Omori** | Under-rated central-ish areas | Good balance of cost and access |
| **Avoid for value:** Shibuya, Shinjuku (except dorm rooms), Nakameguro, Daikanyama | Premium branding | You pay for the postcode, not the commute |

Rule of thumb from the share-house listings: the **same ¥60,000** buys a room in Koenji with a 12-minute Shinjuku commute, or in central Shibuya as a bunk in an 8-person dorm. Decide which of those two things you actually want.
