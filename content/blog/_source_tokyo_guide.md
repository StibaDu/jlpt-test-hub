- *Tipp : Avoid these places if you're on a budget*
    - *animal cafés (with a few exceptions)*
    - *Robot Restaurant and many of the overly  touristy "cool Japan" venues*
    - *Tokyo Tower and Skytree for panoramic views (Shibuya Sky, Tokyo City View, or Bunkyo Civic Center are preferred alternatives)*
    - *Maiko-ya style tea ceremonies, as most Japanese partake in tea ceremonies only during major events (such as funerals, weddings, and significant Shinto rituals)*
    - *Takeshita-dori as a fashion destination – the side streets of Harajuku and Omotesando are preferable*
    - *Any places that use people on the street to lure you in.*
    
- *Tipp : Akihabara **VS** Nakano Broadway*
    
    **Akihabara**, the otaku capital, is great for giant anime posters, idol billboards, and video games. You'll see cosplayers handing out flyers and shops blaring anime music, selling mainstream figures and manga.
    
    However, for specialized or obscure items, other parts of Tokyo might be better. Akihabara caters to tourists with pricier, mainstream goods and longer lines at stores, cafes, and events.
    
    ---
    
    **Nakano Broadway**, located in Nakano, Tokyo, is like Akihabara 2.0. With shops clustered in a covered arcade, it's convenient for browsing, even in bad weather. The flea-market style layout offers a variety of shops and unique items.
    
    You'll find everything from large anime-themed stores like Mandarake to small custom shops. Items available include manga, anime, idol goods, figurines, rare collectibles, fashion, music, video games, cosplay, and game centers.
    
- *Tipp : Tokyo Skytree **VS*** Tokyo Metropolitan Government
    
    **Tokyo Skytree** involves dealing with crowds, high costs, and limited views due to tourist groups, despite offering two observation decks at 350 and 450 meters respectively, with the higher one providing a stunning sunset view for a price exceeding 3000 yen and requiring queuing in multiple lines.
    
    ---
    
    **Tokyo Metropolitan Government** Building now draws crowds, with peak times seeing lines for the 202-meter observation deck, though the entrance is free and the line moves swiftly, offering ample window space, walking room, and amenities including a café and souvenir stands, with early morning or late-night visits recommended to avoid congestion.
    
- *Tipp : Maid Cafes **VS** Themed Restaurants*
    
    i make it short; Expect to spend about 2000 yen or $20 for 30 minutes at one of these cafes.
    
    ---
    
    For a more enjoyable dining experience with a unique atmosphere and cuisine, themed restaurants offer a variety of options catering to different tastes, such as the One Piece-themed restaurant Baratie, and cafes inspired by iconic characters like Moomin, Pokémon, and others.

- *Tipp :* Check the Time for Last Trains
    
    Tokyo trains don’t run all night. **Local lines stop at all stations but Express or Rapid Express lines won’t. So double check your train platform and time before boarding.**
    
- *Tipp : Keep your Trash*
    
    If you've ever been to Tokyo, you'll notice that trash cans are quite scarce. To manage your waste, it's handy to carry a backpack or an extra plastic bag with you. If you find yourself in need of a trash can, convenience stores often have designated areas for disposing of plastics and small items. Additionally, vending machines sometimes have trash bins nearby for bottles and cans, though this isn't always guaranteed.
