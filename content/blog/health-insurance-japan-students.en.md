## The one-sentence difference

- **Genki Traveler** is **travel insurance**: it pays for *unexpected* things while you're away — accidents, emergencies, sudden illness. Maximum **12 months**.
- **Genki Native** is **international health insurance**: long-term cover for people who *live* abroad, including dental, mental health, maternity and preventive care. Runs indefinitely.

Travel insurance is for the trip. International health insurance is for the life. If you need regular care — check-ups, therapy, a dentist — travel insurance is not the right product, no matter the brand.

## What Traveler does better

It's **much cheaper** and dead simple: sign up in minutes (even already abroad), pay monthly, cancel after month one — unused days get refunded. It covers exactly the risk picture of a short stay: accidents, emergencies, sudden illness, sports injuries, repatriation.

Its honest limits, straight from Genki's "Good to know": a €50 deductible per case, a 14-day emergency-only window if you sign up while already travelling, pre-existing conditions excluded, and no dental, mental-health, maternity or preventive care. For short stays that's fine. For real life in Japan it isn't.

## The NHI layer (you need both, whatever Genki you pick)

If you stay in Japan **longer than 3 months** on any visa, you're legally required to enrol in **National Health Insurance (国民健康保険 / NHI)** at your ward office. Neither Genki product exempts you from that — the trick is knowing what each layer does.

**What nobody tells you about NHI:**

- **The gap:** you're uninsured until the ward office finishes your registration — days or weeks. That's precisely the window Genki covers.
- **Year-2 premium spike:** first-year NHI is cheap (~¥1,500–2,500/mo with no income). From year 2 it's recalculated on your Japanese income — a working-holiday earner at ¥120,000/mo can see ¥8,000–15,000/mo, backdated.
- **Japanese-only claims:** NHI reimbursement paperwork is in Japanese.
- **No coverage abroad:** NHI doesn't follow you on a visa run to Seoul or a trip home. Genki does.
- **Visa risk:** unpaid NHI premiums can block visa renewal. A monthly Genki subscription by itself never leaves you in arrears.

**The stack that works:**

| Your stay | Setup |
|---|---|
| Under 90 days | **Genki Traveler only** |
| Working holiday / up to 12 months | **Genki Traveler + NHI** (NHI for compliance, Traveler for the gap and travel) |
| Student visa, multiple years | **Genki Native + NHI** |
| Expat with family | **Genki Native (+ NHI)** |

NHI is your legal base layer; Genki is the layer that speaks English, covers you worldwide and pays hospitals directly.
