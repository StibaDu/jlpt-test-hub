# JLPT Test Day: What to Expect & Last-Minute Tips

The JLPT itself is predictable — 4 sections, printed time table, same rules in every country. What varies is your stress and preparation habits. Here's the day, dissected.

## The schedule (JLPT runs on Japanese time, everywhere)

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 12:00–13:00 | Check-in, find your desk, turn off all electronics |
| 13:05–13:50 | **Language Knowledge (Vocabulary)** — kanji reading, orthography, fill-in, paraphrase |
| ~14:00–18:10 | **Grammar + Reading** (levels differ; breaks in between) |
| ~15:40–16:10 | Break (you leave the room — no peeking at phone) |
| ~16:40–18:10 | **Listening** — audio plays twice per item; you answer in between |

N5 is much shorter (finishes ~16:30). N1 runs the full day.

## What to bring

- **Voucher / admission slip** (with venue and desk number)
- **ID / residence card** (exactly the ID you registered with)
- **HB pencil or wooden pencil + eraser** — only. Mechanical pencils with lead >0.5 mm are banned in many venues
- **Watch without network features** — no smartwatches; many rooms have a wall clock
- **Water bottle with label removed** (some venues), snacks for the break

## What gets confiscated or marked

- Phones — must be off and bagged; even vibration counts as "on" for some proctors
- Notes, books, dictionaries — not in the room during listening
- Smartwatches, tablets, earbuds (including "just for the time")

## The last-week checklist

1. **Simulate the full test timing** — sit for the real duration once this week
2. **Review every marked kanji from your mistake notebook** — don't learn new kanji this week
3. **Confirm seat number + venue on the voucher** — test centers are larger than you think; some are separate buildings
4. **Plan your train** — arrive 60 minutes early; doors close 5 minutes before each section
5. **Prepare snacks + lunch** — you can't leave between sections and return

## Section tactics

- **Vocabulary:** the fastest points. Do them quickly and bank the easy marks.
- **Grammar:** cloze format — read the full sentence BEFORE looking at options; eliminate the obviously wrong particles first
- **Reading:** time caps you — mark a passage if stuck for >90 seconds and move on; return later
- **Listening:** the audio plays twice. First pass: listen loosely, listen for the question shape. Second pass: catch the detail that answers it. Never blank — guess if needed; JLPT has no penalty for wrong answers

## The night before

No flashcards. No new grammar. Sleep 7+ hours — memory consolidation is what makes the last 3 weeks count.
