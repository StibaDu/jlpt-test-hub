## What Genki Native is

Genki Native is **international health insurance** for people living abroad — nomads, expats and families. You can enter the plan **until age 55**, and once you're in there is **no age limit and no maximum duration**: your cover can last for the rest of your life. Minimum commitment is one month, and you can cancel after that.

Two core promises from Genki's own page:

- **Worldwide** — cover follows you everywhere, and it does not switch off when you cross a border
- **Direct billing** — if you're hospitalized, their 24/7 assistance team monitors your treatment and **pays the hospital directly**; claims on outpatient care get reimbursed in about 2 business days

## Basic vs Premium — what's actually covered

Both plans cover the diagnosis, treatment and recovery of accidents, injuries and illnesses — including **chronic conditions** like cancer and diabetes:

- **Inpatient & outpatient care** — hospital stays, surgery, doctor visits, exams and medication
- **Free choice of doctor and hospital** — no network restrictions
- **Emergency medical transport** and treatment in another country if needed
- **All sports included** — really: climbing, skiing, diving, martial arts. Only professional sport is excluded
- **Cover in your country of citizenship** — you can pick 182-day or full-year cover there, which most expat policies exclude

Cover limits: **Basic up to €1,000,000** per person per year, **Premium up to €3,500,000**.

## What it costs

Per Genki's own pricing example: **Genki Native Basic is €189/month** for a 20–24-year-old **with no deductible**.

The price moves with three levers:
- **Your age** — the younger you join, the cheaper
- **Benefit level** — Basic or Premium
- **Deductible** — €0, €500 or €1,000 per year; a higher deductible lowers the monthly price

So there's real room to tune this: students can cut the monthly bill by picking a deductible, while people who expect check-ups and dental visits can take Premium instead.

## The honest fine print

- **Pre-existing conditions:** covered case-by-case. You'll answer health questions per applicant, and the insurer either accepts as-is, accepts with adjustments, or declines — you can apply and see the result without being forced to sign up.
- **Not a public-health-insurance replacement:** Genki Native cannot replace legally mandated statutory insurance (and US cover is limited).
- **Joining window:** until age 55. After that you can't enter, but you can stay covered indefinitely.

## Japan reality check: Native + NHI together

Japan requires anyone staying **longer than 3 months** to enrol in National Health Insurance (国民健康保険 / NHI) — travel or international insurance does not exempt you from that. NHI pays 70% of standard treatment costs and is your legal base layer.

What NHI is not: cover outside Japan, English-language service, or insurance for the weeks before your registration goes through. That's precisely the role of **Genki Native** — worldwide cover, English support, 2-day reimbursements, and your choice of doctors.

**The sensible stack:** NHI for the legal requirement and local routine care, Genki Native on top for worldwide cover, premium hospitals and everything NHI doesn't do.
