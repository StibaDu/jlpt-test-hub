JLPT N5 Study Plan: Your 30-Day Guide

N5 is the easiest JLPT level — but it's also where most people quit, because they study without a structure. Here's a complete 30-day plan that fits 60–90 minutes per day.

What N5 actually requires

Component What you need
Kanji ~100 characters (numbers, days, basic verbs/nouns)
Vocabulary ~800 words
Grammar Particles (は・が・を・に・で・へ・と・も・から・まで), polite forms, past tense, te-form basics
Reading Short passages: emails, notices, simple paragraphs
Listening Slow, clearly spoken dialogues about daily life

The 30-day plan

Days 1–7: Foundations

  • Daily 20 new vocabulary (hiragana words first)
  • 10 kanji per day: numbers 一二三, time words 今日/明日, people/family, basic verbs
  • Grammar: は vs. が, を, に, で — one particle family per day
  • 15 minutes listening: slow YouTube JLPT N5 playlist

Days 8–14: Structure

  • Daily 15 vocab + 10 kanji (review + new)
  • Grammar: verb conjugation — polite -ます → -ました → て-form
  • Start sentence-building: write 5 sentences daily using that day's patterns
  • First practice test (Learning Mode, no pressure)

Days 15–21: Reading & listening push

  • Daily 15 vocab review + 10 kanji
  • Grammar: adjective conjugations (い/な), particles へ, と, も, から, まで
  • Read real JLPT-style passages: 2 short emails/notices daily
  • Listen without pausing: full mock audio sections

Days 22–27: Simulation

  • Two full 30-question tests (Real Test mode, timed)
  • Analyze every mistake — write down the pattern/word, then drill those exact points
  • Flashcards on your mistakes — spaced repetition locks them in

Days 28–30: Final polish

  • Review your entire mistake notebook
  • One final mock test — target ≥70% (the pass bar is 45/60% but leave margin)
  • Sleep, light review only on the last day

The N5 exam structure you're working toward

Knowing the shell helps you train the right muscles — and it's short:

Section Time Questions Score
Vocabulary (語彙) 25 min 24 (kanji reading, orthography, context, paraphrase) 60 pts
Grammar + Reading 50 min 17 grammar + 10 reading items 60 pts
Listening (聴解) 30 min 17 items (task comprehension, point comprehension, utterances, exchanges) 60 pts

Pass = 80/120 overall and ≥ 38 in every section. That two-sided rule is why listening kills people who are acing vocabulary: they pass overall but drop below the per-section floor.

Common N5 mistakes (and the day to fix each)

Mistake Why it happens Fix on
Confusing は and が Both feel like "topic" — the difference is what counts as new information Days 1–2
Ignoring particles へ and で Textbook teaching order puts them last, but the exam tests them constantly Days 3–4
Using the wrong counter 本/枚/匹/台 — the counters, not the words, trip people Day 5–6
Translating every word Slows reading to a crawl; the question usually needs gist + one detail Day 15+
Blank answers in listening Fear of guessing — wrong answers score zero penalty Day 22 (simulation)
Studying kanji by shape only A kanji without its word context won't survive a week of forgetting every day (flashcards)

Week-by-week kanji list to work through

Week Kanji to cover Sample words
1 Numbers (一 二 三 四 五 六 七 八 九 十 百), 今日・明日・毎日, 人・山 一人・三日・今日・毎日・山中
2 Time words 月・火・水・木・金・土・日 + 曜日, 語・学・校 月曜日・学校・日本語
3 Verbs 食・飲・見・聞・読・書・話 + 行く・来る・帰る 食べる・飲み物・話します
4 Adjectives 大・小・高・安・新・古 + weather 雨・雪・空 大きい・新しい・空気

This is ~100 kanji in 4 weeks at 25/week — with room for review inside the daily slot.

Listening practice without a premium subscription

Real audio beats silent study, and much of it is free:

  • Slow JLPT N5 playlist on YouTube — search "JLPT N5 listening slow"; dozens of full-length mock audio sections
  • NHK News Web Easy — genuine news written at N4/N3 level, with furigana; listen along if your browser reads it back
  • Japanese dramas/movies with Japanese subtitles at 0.75× — trains your ear to real speech speed while still being readable
  • TTS on your own sentences — write 10 sentences a day and let your device read them; you learn to hear the grammar you wrote

The trick: listen without reading the transcript first, and only consult the transcript after. That forces your ear, not your eye, to be the primary channel.

Why simulation matters more than study

Most N5 fails aren't knowledge failures — they're format surprises. The real test wraps the same grammar you know in cloze sentences, notices and dialogues, and that unfamiliar shell eats time. Practice tests train the shell, not just the content.
