Genki Review: The Smartest Health Insurance for Japan

You just landed in Japan. You're uninsured from the moment you clear customs until your ward office processes your NHI registration — which can take days or even weeks. One scooter accident in Shibuya, one bad piece of sashimi, one 2 AM appendicitis — and you're paying ¥100,000+ out of pocket.

Genki closes that gap in 5 minutes, from your phone, in English. No ward office. No Japanese forms. No waiting.

Why Genki is the smartest first move in Japan

1. Active from minute one

Buy it on the airport WiFi before you even reach your hotel. Your coverage starts immediately — not after a 2-hour queue at the 区役所 (ward office).

2. No Japanese required

Signup, policy documents, claims — all in English. NHI forms are Japanese-only, and the ward office staff rarely speak English. Genki removes that barrier entirely.

3. Cancel anytime, no lock-in

Monthly subscription. Arrive in Japan, get Genki on day 1, cancel when you leave (or when your NHI kicks in). No 12-month contract, no cancellation fees, no bureaucratic exit process. NHI, by contrast, retroactively bills you for months you were "supposed to" be enrolled — even if you didn't know about the requirement.

4. Follows you across borders

NHI drops the moment you leave Japan. Genki covers your visa run to Seoul, your weekend in Taiwan, your trip home for Christmas — all under one policy. One subscription, global coverage.

5. Built by nomads, for nomads

This isn't a 200-year-old insurance company trying to look modern. Genki was designed mobile-first from the ground up: manage your policy from an app, submit claims with a photo, chat with support in English. The entire experience feels like a fintech product, not a bureaucracy.

6. Covers the NHI gap

Even after you register for NHI (which is mandatory for 3+ month stays — see below), you still pay 30% out of pocket for every visit. Genki can cover that remaining 30% as a supplement, meaning a ¥10,000 hospital visit costs you ¥0 instead of ¥3,000.

The NHI reality check (mandatory but flawed)

If you stay in Japan longer than 3 months on any visa, you're legally required to enroll in National Health Insurance (国民健康保険 / NHI) at your ward office. You cannot opt out — even if you have Genki or any other travel insurance.

Here's what nobody tells you about NHI:

  • The gap: You're uninsured between arrival and NHI registration. That gap can be days (busy ward office) or weeks (missing documents). Genki covers this period.
  • Year-2 premium spike: First-year premiums are low (~¥1,500–2,500/mo for zero-income students). But in year 2, NHI is retroactively recalculated based on your previous year's income in Japan. A working-holiday earner making ¥120,000/mo could see premiums jump to ¥8,000–15,000/mo — backdated.
  • Japanese-only claims: NHI reimbursement forms are in Japanese. If you overpay or need a refund, you're navigating Japanese bureaucracy alone.
  • No coverage outside Japan: Leave for a visa run or a trip home? NHI doesn't follow you. Genki does.
  • Visa renewal risk: Unpaid NHI premiums can block your visa renewal at Immigration. Genki's monthly subscription never puts you in arrears.

The smart combination: Genki from day 1 (instant coverage, no gap) + NHI once your ward office processes the paperwork (for legal compliance). Genki as your safety net, NHI as your legal base layer.
