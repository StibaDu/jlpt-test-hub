Health Insurance in Japan for Students & Digital Nomads

Visa rules and hospital bills collide quickly in Japan — here's how coverage actually works for each visa type.

The mandatory system (National Health Insurance)

If you stay in Japan longer than 3 months on any visa, you're legally required to enroll in the National Health Insurance (国民健康保険 / NHI) at your ward office:

  • Cost: ~¥1,500–2,500/month for students (income-based)
  • Covers: ~70% of medical costs at clinics and hospitals
  • Where: sign up at your ward office (区役所 / kuyakusho) right after registering your address

For students on a student visa, this is cheap and mandatory — and accepted everywhere.

Travel/flexible insurance for short stays & nomads

If you're staying under 3 months, working remotely or moving between countries, Japanese public insurance doesn't apply. That's where global travel health insurance comes in.

Genki — Health Insurance for Digital Nomads

  • Pay-as-you-go: monthly subscription, cancel anytime
  • Designed for visa runs: covers Japan, works while traveling between countries
  • English-native product: signup, docs and claims fully in English — rare among insurers
  • Price ballpark: from ~¥4,500/month depending on age and coverage tier

When Genki-type insurance beats the local system

Your situation Best choice
Student visa, 6+ months Japanese NHI (mandatory anyway)
Tourist/short stay <90 days Travel insurance like Genki
Working holiday, 12 months NHI (register after your address)
Digital nomad hopping between countries Genki or similar nomad insurance
